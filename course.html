Course description

Designed as an introduction to modern geometry for third-year mathematics students, "From Calculus to Cohomology" bridges the gap between classical multivariable calculus and modern differential geometry and topology. Taking the line and surface integrals of calculus as a starting point, the course begins by recasting classical theorems into the elegant language of differential forms and exterior algebra.

From there, we transition to the rigorous study of differentiable manifolds, covering submanifolds, tangent spaces, vector fields, and integration on manifolds.

A central focus of the course is de Rham cohomology. Students will explore its homotopy invariance, the Mayer-Vietoris sequence, and Poincaré Duality, applying these tools to geometric and topological problems. The latter part of the semester delves into advanced topics, including mapping degrees, the Poincaré-Hopf theorem, an introduction to Morse theory, and the differential geometry of vector bundles.

The course culminates in the study of characteristic classes and the generalized Gauss-Bonnet-Chern theorem.

These mathematical structures are not only of foundational importance in pure mathematics, but they also drive modern mathematical physics. For instance, the renowned Yang-Mills gauge theory in high-energy physics is fundamentally described by the differential geometry of vector bundles—a major focal point of our later lectures.

Prerequisites

Solid foundations in Mathematical Analysis and Advanced Algebra; familiarity with basic point-set topology.
